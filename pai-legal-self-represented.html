Start where you are

It recognizes the trouble people actually come in with.

Tell it what kind of case you have, and it explains what cases like that usually turn on. Then it checks your own documents against that list.

Insurance claim denied or underpaid

It usually turns on the policy itself, what you told the insurer and when, an estimate of what the loss cost, and every letter where they denied, delayed or underpaid.

You've been served with a lawsuit

Get the summons, the complaint and the deadline in front of you. Ignoring them can mean a default judgment against you.

Eviction or housing dispute

Keep the notice, the lease, your payment records and every message together, in date order, and ready to show.

Debt collector

Keep the collector's letters and the court papers apart from your own records, so you can see what they've actually shown.

Small claims, custody or support

Turn a stack of papers into a timeline and a list of what still needs proof.

A professional mishandled your case

The original dispute and the one that followed stay in one folder as two separate matters, with the links between them recorded.

The sentence nobody tells you in time

Saying it in your filing isn't proof.

Many people lose because something they wrote in their own court papers was never backed up by an outside document. PAi Legal finds each of those spots and says so directly.

Then it turns each gap into an errand: you still need proof of what it cost you, or proof they owed you something, or proof of when it happened. It also shows where you made the statement, so you can see which of your own words is carrying weight it can't carry.

What you get

Everything a lawyer's file has, explained in your words.

Your documents, named for what they are

"Your main court filing," "the other side's written answer," "something the judge decided." The most important papers are still read first, because a court order and an advertisement aren't equals, whatever the file is called.

Filed or not filed, from the evidence

The app decides whether a case is filed from a docket number, a file stamp, service or an order. It doesn't go by the file name. A stamped complaint in a folder called "draft" is still filed.

It sends you to the real rules

Deadlines, fees and filing rules vary by state, county and sometimes judge. The app doesn't calculate deadlines or guess at rules. Its filing check catches mechanical omissions and tells you to confirm local rules, fees, service and every due date with your court.

Something to hand to legal aid

Export your case as a PDF in plain English. An organized file helps a legal aid lawyer or court navigator understand your situation quickly.

Straight talk

  • PAi Legal isn't a lawyer. It doesn't represent you, file for you, or tell you what to do.
  • It won't hide a weakness in your case to make you feel better. That's why you should run it before you file.
  • It can't predict what a judge will decide.
  • If you can get a lawyer or legal aid, do. Our free directory at yprivateai.com can help you find one.
